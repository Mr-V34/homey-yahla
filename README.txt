YAHLA keeps watch over your home network. It reads the logs your router sends - UniFi, ASUS (Asuswrt and Merlin) or OpenWrt - and tells you the moment a device you have not approved joins your network: a new phone, an unknown laptop on the guest Wi-Fi, or something plugged into a free network port. Approve the devices you know once, and from then on only strangers raise an alert. UniFi has been tested with real equipment; ASUS and OpenWrt are built from published log samples and not yet tested with a real router. TP-Link Deco, AVM Fritz!Box and Netgear home routers cannot send their logs, so they do not work.

YAHLA works on your Homey alone, with no cloud and no extra permissions. Your approvals are protected by a PIN, and YAHLA tells you when something is wrong with itself: when the logs stop, when someone floods it with fake events, or when its settings are changed behind its back. Alerts arrive in the timeline and in your Flows, so you decide whether a stranger turns on the lights, sends a push message or sounds the alarm.
